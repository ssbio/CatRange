From classifier to calibrated ranges

Evolution of this work

Two model generations, built to make mutation effects on enzyme kinetics visible to machine learning.

Foundation

CatLog / KinHub-27k: a curated kinetics dataset

Wild type, mutant, and synthetically inactive sequence–substrate pairs, checked against UniProt/PDB annotations, form the shared experimental backbone both models below are trained on. Explore the dataset itself in CatLog.

2025 preprint · RealKcat

Step 1: teaching a classifier what "broken" looks like

The earlier model, RealKcat, only had to answer yes/no: is this enzyme active? To make it notice mutations that matter — not just any sequence change — the team took real enzyme sequences and swapped out their known catalytic residues (the exact amino acids that do the chemistry) for alanine, a small, chemically inert amino acid. These "switched-off" variants were labeled a new "Cluster 0" while keeping their real experimental KM values.

~5,000 initial switched-off variants expanded to ~17,000 using InterPro annotations

Read the RealKcat preprint (bioRxiv, Feb 2025) · RealKcat codebase

2026 · Published in PNAS Nexus · CatRange

Step 2: from "is it broken?" to calibrated kinetic ranges

CatRange goes further than a simple on/off label. Swapping a catalytic residue for alanine doesn't always kill activity completely — it depends on the enzyme, the residue, and the substrate. So the team added 5,278 of these catalytic-site alanine variants to the training data, and instead of guessing their effect, calibrated it using 22 real published wild-type-vs-mutant measurements as anchors. Those 22 anchor pairs were kept separate from model training and were at most 60% similar to anything the model trained on, so the model couldn't just memorize the answer. Some of those real mutations mostly slowed the enzyme down, some mostly changed how much substrate it needed, and some barely changed anything — CatRange had to learn to tell these apart instead of assuming every catalytic mutation acts the same way.

5,278 calibrated catalytic-site variants 22 real anchor measurements for calibration ≤60% similarity cutoff (no memorizing)

Today

What you actually run

That calibrated model is what runs by default — as Mechanistic Mutation-Aware mode — in both the Colab notebook and the lab's hosted queue, across all four input modes above. You don't need to do anything special to use it; it's simply the default.
